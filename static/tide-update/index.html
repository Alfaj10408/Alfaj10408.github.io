TIDE progress update

Prepared for the supervisor meeting. Every number comes from a stored result file in the repository (branch ablations-v5). Lower mirage rate is better throughout.

In short: TIDE (Grounding then C-Safety) cuts the image-aware mirage rate on the frozen v5 TEST tier for all six model families judged so far, from 0.45 to 0.71 at baseline to 0.06 to 0.18. The ablation campaign shows the gain needs image-conditioned counterfactual supervision, holds up across training recipes and rewording, and costs false abstention on real images. That cost is the main open issue.

2. What the ablations show (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, seed 2, EVAL tier)

The ablations run on the 500-group EVAL tier (2,000 rows), judged with the response-only judge U plus real-image accuracy. TEST was never used for them.

2.1 The safety trade-off

  • Image-conditioned supervision is necessary (A5). Training on generic refusals over the real image gives 0.793, against 0.064 for C-Safety. The model has to learn from counterfactual images.
  • Choice of negatives does not matter much (A6, A7). Random negatives (0.065) and a shared decline target (0.059) match the production recipe (0.064).
  • The recipe is robust (B12 to B06). Training length, LoRA rank, learning rate, decline wording and counterfactual mix all keep the mirage rate between 0.041 and 0.111. Three different settings (1,600 steps, rank 16, a 1:1:2 counterfactual mix) reach the base model's false-abstention level (about 0.08) at a mirage rate of about 0.10. These EVAL results are descriptive, so no setting is selected from them.
  • Decline wording has no measurable effect (B11). Five decline templates perform the same as one.
